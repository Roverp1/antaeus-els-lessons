Lesson 15 · Beginner · Large numbers and amounts

Many and a lot of

Как сказать «много» и спросить о количестве.

Describe large numbers and amounts. Ask about them using familiar sentences.

Выбираем many или a lot of и сохраняем знакомые noun forms и verb forms.

3. Say that the quantity is large

You know how to say that something is available. Sometimes you also want to say that there is a large number or amount.

There are some tools in the garage. We do not give the number.

There are a lot of tools in the garage. We say that the number is large.

Some не называет точное количество. Оно не обязательно маленькое. A lot of прямо сообщает: много.

A lot of: tools and equipment

Use a lot of before a plural countable noun or an uncountable noun. Keep the whole expression: a lot of.

There are a lot of tools. Tools are separate items.

There is a lot of equipment. Equipment is uncountable.

The noun controls is/are: tools → are; equipment → is. The word lot does not change that choice.

A lot of tools = много инструментов. A lot of equipment = много оборудования.

Many: use a plural countable noun

Many also means a large number. Use it with plural countable nouns: many cars, many tools, many appointments.

It is common in questions about the number of things:

Are there many cars at this garage?

В этом автосервисе много машин?

For uncountable nouns, use a lot of in today's patterns:

Is there a lot of water in the bucket?

В ведре много воды?

Not many: the number is not large

There are not any appointments this afternoon. There are none.

There are not many appointments this afternoon. The number is not large.

Not any: совсем нет. Not many: немного; это не сообщает точное число и не обязательно означает ноль.

With an uncountable noun, not a lot of says the amount is not large:

There is not a lot of water in the bottle.

Usage note: a lot of also works in plural questions and negatives. Many is possible in positive statements too. In everyday positive statements, a lot of is a useful default.

Tutor: supported practice

Use Section 4 as a brief bridge, then give most practice time to full sentences. Check the first four Section 5 attempts with the reference available. If agreement or noun number repeats, repair that choice before adding the action-verb transfer. This is a supported-practice check, not evidence of retention.

Model the action examples before assigning homework Level 4. If you defer the transfer, leave that level for a later assignment. Keep many and a lot of out of ambiguous one-answer gaps: both work with plural nouns. Not many is compatible with zero; it does not explicitly report zero as not any does.

6. Drying the car

Read the advice. Choose A, B, or C. Ask about words you need help with.

towel = полотенце; dry the car = вытереть машину насухо.

Reading help

a microfiber towel = полотенце из микрофибры; do not press hard = не нажимайте сильно; soak up most of the water = впитать основную часть воды; small wet spots = небольшие мокрые участки.

from the top down = сверху вниз; a gentle stream of water = несильная струя воды.

At the end of the wash, rinse the car from the top down with a gentle stream of water. Then dry it with a microfiber towel. Do not press hard. Use the towel to soak up most of the water. Use a second towel to dry the small wet spots.

Adapted from steps 6-7 of Brett & Kate McKay's car-washing guide.

  1. What is the advice mainly about?
    1. Washing a car with soap
    2. Drying a car with towels
    3. Fixing a car with tools
  2. What does the first towel soak up?
    1. Most of the water
    2. Soap in the sponge
    3. Water in the buckets
  3. Why use a second towel?
    1. To wash the car again
    2. To rinse the sponge again
    3. To dry small wet spots
Answers

1. B   2. A   3. C

Tutor: reading

Model towel and dry in context before reading. Recycle rinse and wet; check their meanings rather than assuming recall. Gloss a blocking expression briefly, then let the student reread. Accept a choice, pointing to evidence, or a short Russian explanation.

The source gives advice, not a personal story. Imperatives, most of, and longer expressions are input support. The new active senses are towel as a noun and dry as a verb. Do not assess an adjective/verb contrast for dry.

Yes, I have ... / No, I do not.

I can bring ... Я могу принести или привезти ...

After feedback, Leo changes one fact. Ask what you need to know and make a new plan.

Tutor: Leo's card and speaking procedure

Keep this card on your own screen.

ItemWhat Leo has
watersome water
car soapno car soap
bucketstwo buckets
spongesone sponge
towelsno towels
  1. State the goal and what counts as a finished plan. Model an unrelated exchange about gloves. Briefly model We can use ... and I can bring ... as whole expressions if needed. They are speaking support, not a modal-grammar test.
  2. Check the procedure: Whose garage does your card describe? What do we need to decide? Russian clarification is fine. Allow about a minute to inspect the card and choose an opening question; keep the help available.
  3. Play Leo for a short exchange. Each person answers about their own garage. Ask questions too, but leave room for the student to initiate and choose the order. Give useful, brief answers and allow processing time. Accept relevant short answers.
  4. Reach a plan. At the student's garage, Leo brings two buckets. At Leo's garage, the student brings soap and two towels. Either plan works. Other arrangements work if they supply all required items.
  5. Give one specific positive observation and work on one or two useful form points. Clarify misunderstandings immediately during the exchange; use brief corrections when helpful, and let the student reuse them. Avoid correcting every error.
  6. For the replay, change Leo's towels from none to two. Say that one fact has changed without naming it. Keep the roles. Ask again and agree on a plan: Leo's garage now needs only the student's soap; the student's garage still needs Leo's buckets. Let the student choose whether to keep or change the first plan.

Observe the outcome, relevant questions and responses, intelligibility and repair, and one or two familiar forms. Note independent questions, use of visible help, and teacher prompting separately. Many/a lot of is assessed in controlled practice; the exchange does not require it.

Use about 12 minutes for setup, preparation, the first exchange, feedback, and replay. If the first exchange needs longer, keep the feedback and use a shorter replay. The unfinished Lesson 14 activity gives no evidence that the student found speaking difficult.
