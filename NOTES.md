# Tutor notes

Student-specific preferences and observations that steer how lessons are built.

## Student

- Russian L1, adult learner (high-school supplementary tutoring)
- Some English vocab already, understands basic sentences
- Real bug: subject-dropping (inherited from Russian) — not a morphology problem
- Interested in cars (primary), technology (secondary)
- Had 2 lessons so far; lesson 2 covered present simple but poorly

## Teaching rules (self-imposed)

- Always make the student write grammar notes down himself. Lesson 2 failed because I only explained verbally and he didn't record anything.
- Grammar presented as lecture-notes-ready tables/boxes that transfer to paper.
- Language: English explanations with Russian glosses on pronouns and vocab. Grammar terms stay in English.
- Car vocabulary used as the teaching context wherever possible.
- Verbs for present-simple-actions; adjectives and people-nouns for to be. Match vocab to the grammar being taught.
- Teach American English. English File Elementary uses BrE; follow its grammar progression, but swap BrE words/spellings for AmE when introducing vocab. Watch for: tyre/tire, petrol/gas, boot/trunk, bonnet/hood, lorry/truck, motorway/highway, autumn/fall, lift/elevator, indicators/turn signals, car park/parking lot.
- new topics should start with explanation why the topic is needed. I.e. we should explain these questions when starting present simple: why do we need present simple, what are the tenses, what are they used for, what function the accomplish, etc

## Syllabus

- English File Elementary as the spine. File 1 = to be. Don't skip ahead.

## Lesson system

- New lessons and reference sheets load vendored Pico CSS v2 from `assets/pico.min.css`, then course overrides from `assets/course.css`.
- New materials use no more than two typefaces: Arial for interface and headings, Georgia for reading text.
- Legacy materials are isolated under `legacy/`. Do not use their styling as a template for new work.
- New materials must remain responsive and print-friendly.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- Teaching school-required topics (quantifiers) before the foundation is there.
- Assuming exposure = learning. Wait for evidence in learning-records before advancing.
- Letting his existing vocab mask his grammar gaps (he knows word meanings, can't build sentences).
