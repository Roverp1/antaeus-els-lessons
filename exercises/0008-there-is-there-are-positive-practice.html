Things in the garage

Предметы в гараже

Build the new pattern first, then combine it with grammar from earlier lessons. Use the positive there is / there are reference when you need it.

Level 1: choose the opening

Write there is or there are. The noun phrases are already complete.

  1. ______ a handbag on the back seat.
  2. ______ two rings on the floor.
  3. ______ a scarf under the front seat.
  4. ______ three necklaces in the office.
  5. ______ a helpful mechanic at the garage.
  6. ______ four buttons under the jacket.
Sample answers

1. There is   2. There are

3. There is   4. There are   5. There is   6. There are

Level 2: match both number signals

Complete the opening or noun phrase. Make both parts show the same number.

  1. There ______ a handbag on the back seat.
  2. There ______ two ______ on the floor. (ring)
  3. There is ______ scarf under the front seat.
  4. There are three ______ in the office. (necklace)
  5. There ______ an earring beside the handbag.
  6. There ______ four ______ under the jacket. (button)
Sample answers

3. a   4. necklaces   5. is   6. are; buttons

Level 4: one garage, three sentence systems

At closing time, Mia checks Lena's Honda. Mia helps with the lost items, and Lena says that a necklace is not hers. Complete each line of the update. One blank may need more than one word.

  1. ______ a handbag on the back seat.
  2. Mia ______ helpful.
  3. ______ two rings on the floor.
  4. The necklace ______ not hers.
  5. Mia ______ each car before it leaves the garage. (check)
  6. The Honda does not ______ a new engine. (need)
  7. ______ Lena have an appointment? Yes, ______ ______.
  8. ______ the car ready? No, ______ ______ ______.
Sample answers

1. There is a handbag on the back seat.

5. Mia checks each car before it leaves the garage.

7. Does Lena have an appointment? Yes, she does.

2. Mia is helpful.

3. There are two rings on the floor.

4. The necklace is not hers.

6. The Honda does not need a new engine.

8. Is the car ready? No, it is not.

Level 6: read a real kind of message

This workplace message uses natural English, including some grammar from later lessons. Complete only the five numbered gaps. Words in parentheses are basic forms.

Hi Leo,

Quick update on the black Honda. There (1) ______ (be) a handbag on the back seat, and there (2) ______ (be) two (3) ______ (ring) on the floor. I found a scarf under the front seat as well.

The customer says the handbag and scarf (4) ______ (be) hers, but the rings aren't. She (5) ______ (have) an appointment tomorrow afternoon, so she'll pick up her things then. Could you put the handbag and scarf in a bag and leave the rings in the office for now?

Thanks,
Mia

Sample answers

1. is   2. are

3. rings   4. are   5. has

Understand the message

  1. Which car is the message about?
    1. The black SUV
    2. The black Honda
    3. The white Honda
  2. Where is the scarf?
    1. On the back seat
    2. Inside the garage office
    3. Under the front seat
  3. Which things belong to the customer?
    1. The handbag and scarf
    2. The rings and necklace
    3. The jacket and gloves
  4. When is the customer coming?
    1. Tomorrow morning
    2. Tomorrow afternoon
    3. This afternoon
  5. What should Leo leave in the office?
    1. The black handbag
    2. The striped scarf
    3. The two rings
Sample answers

1. B   2. C

3. A   4. B   5. C

Level 7: update another mechanic

You are finishing work at West Street Garage. Leo works tomorrow morning. Write him a natural message of 3-5 sentences. Tell him what is in one customer's car. He also needs to know if the car is ready and if the owner has an appointment. Choose the objects, numbers, facts, and sentence structures yourself. You may invent the details.

Check before sending:

  • The message has 3-5 complete sentences.
  • Leo knows what is in the car.
  • Leo knows whether the car is ready.
  • Leo knows whether the owner has an appointment.
  • The message is clear enough for a coworker to act on it.

Sources

The grammar forms follow Cambridge Grammar: There is, there's and there are. The reading scenario is adapted from a discussion among auto detailers about belongings found in customer cars. The wording and characters are original.
