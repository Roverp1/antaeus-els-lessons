# State after lesson 2: present simple exposure, no retention

Student has had two lessons. Lesson 1 introduced basic sentences with some
vocab; lesson 2 attempted present simple. He understands spoken simple
sentences and has some core vocabulary (drive, start, work, need, etc.),
and can write a rough routine sequence.

The real gap is **not** present simple morphology. His lesson-2 homework
("I get out of bed / In the morning I drink coffee / And then I go to the
car / Go to work / Then I come home, wash up and go to sleep") shows he can
form roughly correct present simple statements. The actual errors:

1. **Subject-dropping** ("Go to work" with no "I") — Russian L1 interference.
   Russian allows subject-less sentences when the verb form implies it
   ("Иду на работу"); English does not. This explains every gap in his
   homework except the last line, which is correct English (subjects drop
   after a conjunction: "I come home, wash up, and go to sleep").

2. **No grammar notes were ever written down**. The tutor explained present
   simple verbally in lesson 2 and didn't instruct the student to record
   the rule. Result: he has nothing to study from, nothing to anchor the
   rule when he's writing alone.

## Implications

- Re-start from "to be" (English File Elementary File 1). Force the
  subject pronoun into every sentence: you cannot say "Am a driver".
- This drills the I/you/he/she/it/we/they frame that the subject-drop bug
  attacks — to be is the cleanest vehicle to fix it.
- Every lesson must include explicit "write this down" instruction for the
  grammar block. Verbal-only explanation has been tried and failed. This is
  refined by LR-0005: notes should be concise and reconstructed in stages,
  not copied as long blocks.
- Don't advance to quantifiers (some/any/many/a lot of) on the school's
  demand until to be, present simple actions, there is/are, and plurals
  are all evidenced as learned. Quantifiers decorate these, they don't
  replace them.

## Evidence

- Lesson-2 homework submission (preserved verbally in lesson plan).
- Tutor's self-report that the lesson did not land well and no notes were
  taken during the explanation.
